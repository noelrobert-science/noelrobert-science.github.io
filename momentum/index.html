Short handwritten checkpoint · show or upload

One short paper checkpoint · about 5 minutes

Stopping a ball. A 0.20 kg ball travels to the right at 6.0 m·s−1 and is brought to rest in 0.30 s. Take right as positive.

  1. Show your calculation of the ball's change in momentum, including its sign and unit.
  2. Calculate the average net force on the ball. State its direction.
  3. In one short sentence, explain what happens to the average force magnitude if the same ball stops in twice the time.

What stays the same?

Start with two carts that stick together. Predict their final motion before revealing the answer.

Change either mass: move the Mass slider under Cart A or Cart B. Change one cart at a time, then run the collision again.

Try these three investigations

  1. Choose stick together, equal masses and a stationary target. What happens to the speed?
  2. Keep the first cart unchanged and increase the target's mass. Predict, run and compare.
  3. Try elastic bounce. Does the total momentum still stay the same?

The idea to take away

The total linear momentum of an isolated system remains constant. Each cart can change momentum while the total stays the same.

Here, net external horizontal force is zero. Kinetic energy is conserved in an elastic collision; in a stick-together collision some becomes internal energy, sound and deformation.

Why are the velocities signed?

Right is positive and left is negative. Momentum is a vector, so direction matters. Add signed momenta, not just their sizes.

Explain it in words

For the same change in momentum, a longer interaction time gives a smaller magnitude of average net force:

Fnet,avg = ΔpΔt

The rectangle represents an equivalent constant average force. A real force can vary during contact; the signed area under its force–time graph is still the impulse.
