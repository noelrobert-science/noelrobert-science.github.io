Definitions to learn: heating and cooling (p 41)

Heating a substance: When a solid or liquid is heated, the particles gain energy and vibrate faster and faster until they have enough energy to break free from the forces of attraction between the particles.

Cooling a substance: When a gas or liquid is cooled, the particles lose energy and move more slowly. The particles can no longer overcome the forces of attraction and move closer together (liquid) or vibrate in a fixed position (solid).

Definitions to learn: changes of state (p 42)

Melting: When a solid changes into a liquid, melting takes place. The temperature at which a substance melts is its melting point.

Freezing: When a liquid changes into a solid, freezing takes place. The temperature at which a substance freezes is its freezing point.

Vaporisation: When a liquid changes into a gas, vaporisation takes place. There are two types of vaporisation: evaporation and boiling.

Condensation: When a gas changes into a liquid, condensation takes place.

Sublimation: When a solid changes directly into a gas, without going through the liquid phase, sublimation takes place.

Deposition: When a gas changes directly into a solid, without going through the liquid phase, deposition takes place.

Finding the state at a given temperature (p 50)
  1. Write down the melting point (mp) and the boiling point (bp). Draw a quick number line if the numbers are negative.
  2. Temperature below the mp: solid.
  3. Temperature between the mp and the bp: liquid.
  4. Temperature above the bp: gas.
  5. Exactly at the mp: melting or freezing (solid and liquid together). Exactly at the bp: boiling (liquid and gas together).
