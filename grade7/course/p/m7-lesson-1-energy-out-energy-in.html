🌡️ Exothermic or endothermic? · pp 177–178
Temperature rises
Temperature falls
Exothermic reaction
A reaction that releases energy into the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings increases. The products have less energy than the reactants.
Endothermic reaction
A reaction that absorbs energy from the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings decreases. The products have more energy than the reactants.
System and surroundings · p 190
System: the reacting chemicals. Surroundings: everything else (water, beaker, air, your hand). The thermometer measures the surroundings.
💡 EXothermic: energy EXits (leaves) the reaction, and the thermometer reading goes up. ENdothermic: energy ENters the reaction, and the thermometer reading goes down.
Common mistake (p 178)

WRONG: An endothermic reaction feels hot because it absorbs heat.
RIGHT: An endothermic reaction feels cold. It takes thermal energy from its surroundings, including your hand.
