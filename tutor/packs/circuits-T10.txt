SAGS WORDING (IEB SAGS 2025 (updated Sept 2024), Physical Sciences, E. Electric Circuits, p. 29/36-29/37)
- Solve problems involving current, potential difference and resistance for circuits containing arrangements of resistors in series and in parallel for a maximum of three external resistors.
- State that a real battery has internal resistance.
- Define emf as the total energy supplied per coulomb of charge by the cell.
- Know that the sum of the voltages across the external circuit plus the voltage across the internal resistance is equal to the emf: emf = Vload + Vinternal resistance or emf = I(Rext + r).
- Solve circuit problems in which the internal resistance of the battery must be considered, including series-parallel networks of resistors to a maximum of three external resistors.
- Define potential difference as the work done per unit positive charge: V = W/q.
- Define current as the rate of flow of charge: I = q/t.
- Determine the relationship between current and potential difference at constant temperature.
- State Ohm's Law: The current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across the conductor at constant temperature.
- Distinguish between ohmic and non-ohmic conductors.
- Know that resistance is a material's opposition to the flow of electric charge. Know that the unit of resistance is the ohm (Ω).
- Calculate the effective resistance of resistors in series using RS = R1 + R2 + ...
- Calculate the effective resistance of resistors in parallel using 1/RP = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ...
- Interpret circuit diagrams containing a source, switches, resistors, ammeters and voltmeters.
- Solve problems using the mathematical expression of Ohm's Law, R = V/I, for series and parallel circuits, limited to a maximum of three external resistors.

COURSEBOOK AND STUDIO LESSON: "emf and r from two readings or a graph" (Module 11, section 11.4)
- A straight line: y = mx + c: emf = V_{load} + Ir can be written V = (−r)I + emf. Plot the terminal pd (y) against the current (x). The gradient is −r and the y-intercept is the emf. Each reading of one battery is one point on that line. (Module 11, section 11.4)
- Check, "On a graph of terminal pd (y) against current (x) for one battery, what is the y-intercept?": The y-intercept is at I = 0: no lost volts, so it is the emf. (Module 11, section 11.4)
- Check, "The line has a gradient of −2,00 V·A⁻¹. What is r?": gradient = −r, so r = 2,00 Ω. Its unit is V·A⁻¹ = Ω. (Module 11, section 11.4)
- Reading the graph: Take the gradient from two points on the best-fit line, far apart and marked on the graph. Never from table values. Extend the line to the y-axis to read the emf. To read off: at a given current, read V from the line, then R = V/I. (Module 11, section 11.4)
- Worked example, Worked example 11.9: the terminal pd against current graph: “A student varies the current through the circuit by varying the external resistance, R … and record the reading on the voltmeter across the battery.” Current (A): 0,8; 2,4; 3,2; 4,4; 5,5; 6,3. Voltmeter (V): 18,7; 15,8; 14,1; 12,6; 10,3; 9,0. 8.1.2 Calculate the gradient. (4) 8.1.3 Calculate the internal resistance. (3) 8.1.4 Without a calculation, determine the emf. (2) 8.1.5 What will R be when the current is 4,0 A? (3) Answer: 8.1.2 Two points on the line: (1,0 A; 18,25 V) and (6,0 A; 9,50 V) ✓ | gradient = Δy/Δx = (9,50 − 18,25)/(6,0 − 1,0) | gradient = −1,75 | unit: V·A⁻¹ (or Ω) ✓ | 8.1.3 V_{load} = emf − Ir, so V = (−r)I + emf | gradient = −r | r = 1,75 Ω | 8.1.4 emf = 20 V ✓. It is the y-intercept: at I = 0 there are no lost volts, so the terminal pd equals the emf ✓. | 8.1.5 emf = I(R + r) | 20 = 4(R + 1,75) | R = 3,25 Ω | Or read off V = 13 V at 4,0 A, so R = V/I = 13/4 = 3,25 Ω. (IEB 2022 P1 Q8.1)
- Check, "A battery is connected first to a 2,0 Ω resistor (current 2,0 A), then to a 1,0 Ω resistor (current 3,0 A). So emf = 2,0(2,0 + r) and emf = 3,0(1,0 + r). Find r.": The same battery has the same emf and r. 4 + 2r = 3 + 3r, so r = 1,00 Ω. (Module 11, section 11.4)
- Check, "Now put r = 1,00 Ω back in. What is the emf?": emf = 2,0(2,0 + 1,0) = 6,00 V. Check: 3,0(1,0 + 1,0) = 6,00 V. (Module 11, section 11.4)
- By the end the learner can: Write V = (−r)I + emf and match it to y = mx + c.; Take a gradient from two points on the line, with its unit.; Read the emf at the y-intercept.; Solve two states of one battery for r and the emf. (Module 11, section 11.4)

EXAM TRAP: The gradient from the table (IEB Nov 2022, Paper 1, Question 8.1.2)
- Question: You have plotted the terminal pd (y-axis) against the current (x-axis) for a battery and drawn the best-fit line. It passes through (1,0 A; 18,25 V) and (6,0 A; 9,50 V). The table's first and last rows are (0,8 A; 18,7 V) and (6,3 A; 9,0 V). Calculate the gradient, and then the internal resistance. (4 + 3)
- The usual wrong answer: Gradient = (9,0 − 18,7)/(6,3 − 0,8) = −1,76 V·A⁻¹, using two rows of the table.
- What went wrong: The gradient belongs to the line, not to the readings. Read two points far apart on your line, mark them on the graph, and use those.
- The memo: Gradient = (9,50 − 18,25)/(6,0 − 1,0) = −1,75 V·A⁻¹. V = (−r)I + emf, so r = −gradient = 1,75 Ω.
- The examiners, word for word: "Use points that lie on the best-fit straight line, rather than points from the table." (IEB Examiners' Report 2022, Paper 1, Question 8.1.2)

COMMON SLIPS the practice engine names (Mr R's practice engine)
- If a learner picks "V·A⁻¹": Gradient = Δy/Δx: here the y-unit is the ampere.
- If a learner picks "Ω": This gradient is 1/R, so its unit is Ω⁻¹.
- If a learner picks "no unit": A gradient keeps the units of its axes.
- If a learner picks "A·V⁻¹": Gradient = Δy/Δx: the y-unit divided by the x-unit.
- If a learner picks "V·A": Divide, do not multiply.
- If a learner picks "A·V⁻¹; the gradient is r": Δy/Δx with V on y: V·A⁻¹. The line slopes down, so the gradient is −r.
- If a learner picks "V; the gradient is the emf": The emf is the y-intercept, not the gradient.
- If a learner picks "V·A⁻¹; the gradient is Rext": The gradient of this graph is −r.

MR R'S MARKING (how the app and Mr R mark calculations)
- Every calculation: formula ✓, substitution ✓, answer with unit ✓. A correct formula and substitution earn marks even when the answer is wrong. (Mr R's marking)
- Follow-through: a wrong answer carried correctly into the next part is not punished twice. (Mr R's marking)
- Start every calculation with a formula: "It is best practice to begin each calculation with an appropriate formula, which will be used to solve the problem." (IEB Examiners' Report 2024, Paper 1)
- Round final answers to 2 decimal places; use a decimal comma. (Mr R's marking)
- A correct formula from the formula sheet earns the formula mark on its own, even before it is rearranged (R = V/I when V is asked) and even when the substitution is wrong. Never call it wrong. (Mr R's marking)
- The answer, its unit and (for a vector) its direction share ONE mark. (Mr R's marking)
- Adding or subtracting currents or pds is first principles: there is no formula-sheet formula for it, so a blank formula line costs nothing there. Full marks come from a correct principle, substitution and answer. RS = R1 + R2 chosen on such a part is accepted: never call it wrong. (Mr R's marking)

EXAMINERS' COMMENTS, PAPER 1 (copied word for word from the IEB NSC Examiners' Reports 2017-2025; a few formulas did not copy cleanly from the PDFs: never quote a formula from this section, only the words)
- IEB Examiners' Report 2017, Paper 1, Question 7 (word for word): ELECTRIC CIRCUITS This question was very well-answered by the majority of candidates. Candidates must learn definitions as stated in the SAGs and ensure they use the correct units for the quantities being calculated. Most candidates who attempted to answer the question using ratios based on the values of the resistors made errors. Please encourage candidates to work from first principles and use the equations supplied on the data sheet correctly.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2018, Paper 1, Question 7 (word for word): ELECTRIC CIRCUITS This question was slightly more demanding than the circuit questions asked in previous papers. A few pointers for the future:  Candidates must learn definitions as stated in the SAGs and ensure that they use the correct units for the quantities being calculated.  Candidates must calculate resistance for resistors in parallel carefully, e.g. many candidates stated that two resistors, each of resistance R, combine to provide an equivalent resistance of 1 2R rather than 1 2R .  Numerical answers must be given as a decimal not a proper fraction.  Candidates must be able to explain their answers logically using physics from first principles.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2019, Paper 1, Question 7 (word for word): ELECTRIC CIRCUITS This question was the highest-scoring question in the paper. A few pointers for the future:
• Candidates must learn definitions as stated in the SAGs and ensure that they use the correct units for the quantities being calculated.
• Candidates must calculate resistance for resistors in parallel carefully and use correct mathematics. E.g. many candidates stated that 1 RP= 1 4 = 4 Ω rather than RP= 4Ω.
• Numerical answers must be given as a decimal, not a proper fraction.
• Candidates must be able to explain their answers logically using physics from first principles. When explaining the decrease in potential difference across the battery, candidates were not careful with their explanation. E.g. many spoke about internal resistance increasing or emf decreasing rather than linking the increase in current to the increase in lost volts and hence the decrease in terminal potential difference.
• Candidates need to understand what a voltmeter across an open switch will measure. Many candidates incorrectly answered 7.1.3 as 0V.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2020, Paper 1, Question 7 (word for word): ELECTRIC CIRCUITS This question was one of the highest-scoring question in the paper. A few pointers for the future:
• Candidates must learn definitions as stated in the SAGs and ensure that they use the correct units for the quantities being calculated. In Ohm's law it should be stressed that current is directly proportional to potential difference and not vice versa. Current depends on potential difference.
• Candidates must calculate resistance for resistors in parallel carefully and use correct mathematics, e.g. many candidates stated: 1 RP= 2 15 = 7,5 Ω instead of RP= 7,5Ω.
• Learners must write the formula they are trying to use and apply it correctly to the part of the circuit they are dealing with. Using subscripts would be useful to help their thinking as well as to aid the markers in following their logic, e.g. R∥, Ittttt, I10Ω
• Numerical answers must be given as a decimal, not a proper fraction.
• Candidates must be able to explain their answers logically using physics from first principles. Please encourage learners to use bullet points to ensure a coherent argument that does not include irrelevant information.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2021, Paper 1, Question 7 (word for word): ELECTRIC CIRCUITS This question considered the current drawn by a pump and the cost of operating the pump in the first part and then a circuit with a bulb and resistor in parallel with one another. This question was generally well answered.
• Cost calculations should be easy marks, but errors were made with conversion from minutes to hours and candidates confused kWh, Wh and kW/h.
• Many candidates confused the emf and the voltage across the bulb. They should use subscripts to show which voltage is being considered.
• Candidates struggled to match the relevant current, voltage and resistance values in each calculation.
• The rate of energy dissipation was not recognised as power.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2022, Paper 1, Question 8 (word for word): ELECTRIC CIRCUITS The first part of this question tested the skill of drawing and using a graph of data in the context of electric circuits.

• While this skill has improved over the years, many candidates are still making fundamental errors: – Points should be clearly visible, but small enough to be accurate. Crosses are recommended (where the cross lies on the exact point). – A single best-fit straight line must be drawn that extends beyond the first and last points that are plotted. – A sensible scale must be used to allow accurate plotting (multiples of 2 or 5). – Use points that lie on the best-fit straight line, rather than points from the table. – If the graph is reattempted, erase the original line completely. The second part of the question was a simple circuit with an unknown resistor and a bulb in parallel with a battery.
• Many candidates used RP = ( 1 R1 + 1 R2 ) –1 which reduced the prevalence of incorrect mathematical statements and forgetting to invert their answer for 1 RP.
• Energy and power were frequently confused.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2023, Paper 1, Question 8 (word for word): ELECTRIC CIRCUITS This question involved a starter motor and headlamps connected to a battery. Part of the question needed to be solved by setting up simultaneous equations and solving them. The latter part of this question also tested the conceptual understanding of the effect of a change to the circuit on the brightness of the bulbs.

• Some candidates showed poor concept of the circuit and how the values relate to one another, for example, calculating the reading on A1 in 8.3, but not dividing this current by 2 to find the current through each headlamp. Or not realising that emf and internal resistance are properties of a cell and remain unchanged when the circuit connected to the cell is changed.
• There was some careless substitution in this question, as well as some incorrect mathematical manipulation.
• Question 8.9 proved challenging for many candidates, requiring them to demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the circuit and the interaction of components.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2024, Paper 1, Question 9 (word for word): ELECTRIC CIRCUITS This question referred to a circuit with a resistor and a bulb connected in parallel to another resistor and a battery with unknown internal resistance.

• Candidates did not all use the correct, 'matched' quantities to answer each question. The use of subscripts or writing values in on the circuit diagram may help candidates to use the appropriate values in their calculations.
• There were many basic errors in this question, such as incorrect mathematical manipulation and incorrect units.
• Question 9.7 proved challenging for many candidates. They did not seem clear in their understanding of the difference between terminal voltage/load voltage and lost volts. Explanations needed to refer clearly to appropriate changes and the impact of these changes. Bullet points may be useful in clearly conveying candidates' thought process.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2025, Paper 1, Question 8 (word for word): ELECTRIC CIRCUITS This question referred to a circuit with resistors that could be connected in parallel or in series by means of two switches to a battery with unknown internal resistance.

• Calculations were well answered, but explanations were poorly answered.
• Many answers were expressed as fractions, which are not accepted, and units were frequently omitted.
• Mathematical coherence remains a problem when the effective resistance of resistors in parallel is determined.
