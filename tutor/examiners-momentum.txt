- IEB Examiners' Report 2017, Paper 1, Question 5 (word for word): MOMENTUM, WORK, ENERGY AND POWER This question consisted of two parts. A question on a block sliding across a rough surface and a question about a ballistic pendulum. This question was well-answered by most candidates. A few pointers for the future:  Candidates must understand the momentum is conserved in an isolated system and the law must be applied to the full system and not to one of the objects in the system. It is also important to know that a closed system is not an equivalent term for an isolated system.  Candidates must remember that momentum is a vector quantity and use of signs to deal with momentum in opposite directions is important.  When calculating change in kinetic energy, candidates must realise that 2 2 v u  is not the same as   2 . v u 
- IEB Examiners' Report 2020, Paper 1, Question 5 (word for word): MOMENTUM, WORK, ENERGY AND POWER The first part of this question was a standard textbook-type question on conservation of momentum and using the work–energy theorem. The definitions were often stated incorrectly, and many candidates tried to solve the momentum question using ∆p= 0, but confused signs. Candidates who used (pttttt)bbbbbb= (pttttt)aaaaa were more successful. When using the work–energy theorem, candidates must ensure they are using the net force and calculating the change in kinetic energy. The second part of this question revealed some common errors which learners must be made aware of. Too many candidates used conservation of mechanical energy but ignored the fact the ball had kinetic energy as well as potential energy at level A. Encourage learners to use (EK+ EP)iiiiiii= (EK+ EP)fffff so they consider all contributions to mechanical energy. Very few candidates were able to answer question 5.2.4 correctly. Most candidates were able to calculate the net force but did not continue to calculate the force that the ground exerts on the basketball. This type of question has appeared a few times in recent years and candidates who read the question carefully and had worked through past papers should have been able to be successful. Candidates should also be guided by the mark allocation: calculating net force was not asked in the question.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2021, Paper 1, Question 5 (word for word): MOMENTUM, WORK, ENERGY & POWER The first part of this question involved a child pushing off the trunk of a tree to swing up to a vertical height on a swing. The second part of the question dealt with an athlete sliding to rest across a field.
• When using impulse to calculate the average net force exerted on the child and also when calculating the change in kinetic energy to apply the work–energy theorem, the most common error was forgetting the vector nature of velocity so that one velocity had to be negative. Another common error was switching the initial and final velocities when finding the difference.
• Some candidates seemed unable to conceptualise the problem to calculate the maximum height reached by the child and candidates should be encouraged to draw a diagram to help them solve a problem. Many candidates attempted to calculate the mechanical energy instead of applying the law of conservation of mechanical energy, while others used the wrong velocity. Equations of motion could not be used as the motion was not linear.
• Many candidates did not answer question 5.2.1 correctly, calculating the change in kinetic energy instead of just the kinetic energy.
• Many candidates were unable to interpret the question that asked for the rate at which the frictional force dissipates the kinetic energy of the athlete. The majority of those who realised that dissipation of energy is power tried to use the formula P = Fv.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2022, Paper 1, Question 5 (word for word): MOMENTUM, WORK, ENERGY & POWER The concepts of conservation of momentum, Newton's third law and impulse were tested in a context where two blocks collided.

• There were a number of routine errors made in answering these questions, such as squaring velocity to calculate momentum, writing incomplete equations or ignoring the vector nature of velocity and not giving a direction.
• Many candidates did not offer an explanation for their answers to questions 5.2 and 5.3 or simply made a statement or showed a calculation without linking it to their answer.
• Some statements were made in answering the questions that showed a lack of understanding, such as mixing up Newton's laws, using the terms 'momentum' and 'change in momentum' interchangeably and incorrectly stating that the net force was equal to change in momentum (not the rate of change of momentum).
- IEB Examiners' Report 2022, Paper 1, Question 6 (word for word): MOMENTUM, WORK, ENERGY & POWER A coin was pushed along a surface and the questions tested knowledge of forces and the work–energy theorem. This question was very well answered by many candidates.

• Many candidates did not correctly specify in their formula which force was acting (frictional force or the horizontal component of the applied force or the net force) and candidates are encouraged to use subscripts in their formulae to help them to use the correct value and to make their understanding evident.
• Incorrect conversion of grams and centimetres was a problem for many candidates.
• Some candidates used vertical and horizontal forces together in their vector sums.
- IEB Examiners' Report 2023, Paper 1, Question 6 (word for word): MOMENTUM, WORK, ENERGY & POWER In the first part of this question, a ball was kicked against a wall and bounced, and in the second part of the question the ball broke a glass door.

• Impulse was poorly defined by many candidates.
• The change in direction of the ball was often disregarded in the mathematics of Question 6.2 and candidates are encouraged to draw a sketch diagram to help them to remember to assign a positive direction. The change in velocity must always be calculated by final velocity minus initial velocity.
• A significant number of candidates squared the change in velocity when using the work- energy theorem (using 1 2 m(v−u)2 instead of 1 2 mv2 − 1 2 mu2 or 1 2 m(v2 −u2)).
- IEB Examiners' Report 2025, Paper 1, Question 5 (word for word): MOMENTUM, WORK, ENERGY AND POWER A block with unknown mass slid to rest across a surface. Candidates needed to work with expressions in terms of the unknown mass of the block to solve the questions asked.

• Candidates struggle to write an expression in terms of an unknown, most likely because all the required values are usually given.
• Working with the expression for change in kinetic energy continues to challenge candidates mathematically.
• Forgetting to apply the correct signs for vector quantities and not using the SI unit for time (s) were also common errors.
