Explained example

Problem: Their foam supplier raises prices by 15%. Which environment is this, and how much control does Sole Revival have?

Step 1, who is it? A supplier, a business Sole Revival deals with directly. That is the market environment.

Step 2, control? Ayanda cannot order the supplier to drop its price, but she can negotiate, buy in bulk or find another supplier. That is some influence, not control.

Step 3, ripple: The supplier may have raised prices because the fuel price rose (macro). The macro change pushed on the market, and now Sole Revival must respond inside its micro environment.

Say it like an examiner

Micro environment: the internal environment of the business: its functions, resources, vision and mission, policies, structure and culture. Management has full control.

Market environment: customers, competitors, suppliers, intermediaries and other organisations the business deals with directly. The business can influence but not control it.

Macro environment: the political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental forces that affect all businesses. The business has no control.

Theme lens · IEB 2026 theme

Sit-down dining

Each year IEB gives matrics an industry theme for the final exam. In 2026 it is "Sit-Down" Dining Businesses. Examiners want examples from the theme, not generic ones. Practise the habit now.

Your mission

Choose a sit-down restaurant you know, such as a Spur, a Mugg & Bean, an Ocean Basket or a local family restaurant. Give one factor for each PESTLE letter, then name the two strongest of Porter's forces.

Need a start?
Show example factors

P: liquor licence rules · E: food prices, interest rates · S: families eating out less, health trends · T: online booking, delivery apps · L: health and safety laws, BCEA for waiters · E: load-shedding, water shortages.

Strong forces: rivalry (many restaurants in one mall); substitutes (Uber Eats and Mr D bring food home).
