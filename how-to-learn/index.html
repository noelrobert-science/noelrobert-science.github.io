How to learn (anything)

If it feels easy, it probably isn't sticking.

Rereading and highlighting feel productive, but they don't build memory. What does is having to pull it out, write it, and struggle a little.

  • Test yourselfCover it, write it, then check. Pulling it out of your memory is what puts it there.
  • Come back laterReturn after a few days, then a few weeks. A bit of forgetting first makes it stronger.
  • Mix it up Grade 9 and upPractise different kinds of questions together, so you learn to choose the method and not just repeat it.
  • Copy, then tryStudy a worked example, finish a half-done one, then do one alone.
  • Check your confidenceBefore you mark, say "sure" or "unsure". The ones you were sure about and got wrong teach you the most.

On every page of our books and apps, these habits have a name: Ready?, Quick check, Look back, Try again on, Now you. Now you know why they're there.

Meet me halfway. Before you ask for help, bring your own attempt, written down. Even a wrong one. That's where the learning starts.

These habits work in every subject, for the rest of your life.
